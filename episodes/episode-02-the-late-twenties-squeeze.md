**Couples and money.**
- Joint vs separate accounts (randomised): Olson, Rick, Small & Finkel (2023), "Common Cents: Bank Account Structure and Couples' Relationship Dynamics", *Journal of Consumer Research*. Newlywed/engaged couples assigned to joint, separate, or choice conditions and followed for two years; joint-account couples maintained relationship quality while separate-account couples declined. Dan's caveats (one study, early-relationship sample, transparency as likely mechanism) reflect the authors' own discussion.
- Financial infidelity: Garbinsky, Gladstone, Nikolova & Olson (2020), "Love, Lies, and Money: Financial Infidelity in Romantic Relationships", *Journal of Consumer Research*.
- Money conflict is less frequent but more intense/longer-lasting/less resolved than other topics: Papp, Cummings & Goeke-Morey (2009), "For Richer, for Poorer: Money as a Topic of Marital Conflict in the Home", *Family Relations*.
- Early financial disagreement as a predictor of later divorce, controlling for income and assets: Dew, Britt & Huston (2012), "Examining the Relationship Between Financial Issues and Divorce", *Family Relations*.
- "Proportional split" is a widely recommended practice, not a research finding; presented as Sophie's view with Dan's caveat.

**Friendship and comparison.**
- Social comparison: Festinger (1954), "A Theory of Social Comparison Processes", *Human Relations*.
- Contact network peaks around age 25 then declines: Bhattacharya, Ghosh, Monsivais, Dunbar & Kaski (2016), "Sex differences in social focus across the life cycle in humans", *Royal Society Open Science* (mobile phone data, ~3 million users).
- Salary secrecy / tall poppy framing is cultural observation, not cited research.

**Behavioural economics.**
- Mental accounting ("stuff between mates" bucket): Thaler (1985, 1999). Loss aversion (losses ~2x gains): Kahneman & Tversky (1979), *Econometrica*. The "about twice" figure is the commonly cited approximation and varies by study.
